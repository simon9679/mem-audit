# Review of PROBE_symdiff_probe.md check #3 (semantic label permutation)

This is a post-hoc review of the word-permutation criterion in the original
validation run (`PROBE_symdiff_probe.md`, 2026-08-10, commit `7271912`). It is
written by hand, not generated by the script.

## The criterion and the failure

Check #3 requires strict `paraphrase < word_permutation` at thresholds
0.60, 0.72, and 0.82. The observed values:

| threshold | paraphrase symdiff % | word permutation symdiff % | strict < ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.60 | 0.00 | 0.00 | False (both zero) |
| 0.72 | 0.00 | 0.00 | False (both zero) |
| 0.82 | 18.18 | 0.00 | False (paraphrase higher) |

Two of the three failures are arithmetic: at 0.60 and 0.72 both values saturate
to 0.00, and strict inequality between two equal quantities is false regardless
of the metric's behavior. The third (0.82) is a genuine result: paraphrase is
*more* divergent than word permutation, meaning the metric finds scrambled words
more similar to the original than paraphrases -- the opposite of what the
criterion expected.

## Why the fixture does not test what it claims

The check is titled "semantic label permutation" and the criterion text claims
to test meaning discrimination. But the word-permutation fixture preserves the
exact word set:

```python
import re; tok = lambda s: sorted(re.findall(r'[a-z]+', s.lower()))
sum(tok(a) == tok(b) for a, b in zip(SEMANTIC_ORIGINAL, SEMANTIC_SCRAMBLED))   # 10
sum(tok(a) == tok(b) for a, b in zip(SEMANTIC_ORIGINAL, SEMANTIC_PARAPHRASE))  # 0
```

All 10 of 10 scrambled-original pairs share the exact same word multiset.
Zero of 10 paraphrase-original pairs do. The fixture therefore tests
sensitivity to word order, not sensitivity to meaning. That sentence-embedding
models are largely insensitive to word order is a known property documented in
the literature (Sinha et al., 2021, "Unnatural Language Inference";
ACL 2022.acl-long.476), so the high similarity between scrambled and original
sentences is a correct answer from the model, not a defect.

## Disposition

The original ISSUE verdict in `PROBE_symdiff_probe.md` is **not rewritten**.
The check is retained in the test suite under a narrowed claim: it measures
word-order sensitivity of the embedding model, not meaning discrimination. The
section is renamed from "semantic label permutation" to "word-order sensitivity"
in v2.

The check that does measure meaning discrimination already passed: the
antonym substitution check destroys meaning through negation, and paraphrase was
less divergent than antonym at 0.72 (0.00 vs 18.18) and at 0.82 (18.18 vs
75.00). Both results are in the original document.

## Why the INCONCLUSIVE state is needed

The 0.60 and 0.72 failures in check #3 and several cells in check #2 (antonym)
share the same structure: both values being compared equal 0.0, so strict
inequality is trivially false. This is floor saturation, not a measurement
outcome. The v2 script introduces an `INCONCLUSIVE` state for any comparison
where both operands are 0.0. The rule applies uniformly to all checks,
including the antonym check, not selectively to one.
